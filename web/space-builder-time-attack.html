Space Builder: +RT

What can response times add?

Research preview, not part of the released estimator

This page is us exploring what a joint counts-and-response-time model could look like. The released Python and R packages fit counts only. The RT checkpoint is not bundled, and the simulator below is a simplified ballistic linking model rather than a full LBA. Treat the results as a worked proof of concept, not a ready-made analysis.

The point of this page

A confusion matrix tells you what someone perceived. It cannot tell you how they got there, whether they processed the two dimensions one after another or at the same time, whether they waited for both or stopped as soon as one was settled.

That isn't a limitation of the method. It's a limitation of the data. Below, three of the five architectures produce the same confusion matrix. Not similar, the same. No amount of accuracy data could separate them, however it is analysed. But they can finish at visibly different times. Response times can represent the only channel the information is in.

The per-trial table gets compressed down to 5 quantiles per cell (80 numbers total) before it reaches the model; that compression is shown live in the The response times card below once you run an experiment.

The response times

One curve per stimulus, over the times of correct responses. Each curve is scaled by that stimulus's accuracy, so the area under it is P(correct), a curve that is both low and fast is the signature of guessing, not of 'skill'.

The small ticks under each curve are the five quantiles GRIN actually reads (10th, 30th, 50th, 70th, 90th). That is the entire RT input: 5 numbers per cell, 80 in total. Everything the model concludes about processing, it concludes from those ticks.
