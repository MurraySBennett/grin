Evidence

GRIN's recovery of known values, and uncertainty calibration

What this page is for

The rest of the site analyses one dataset at a time. This page asks two questions across many simulated participants: does GRIN recover the true values, and are its uncertainty ranges the right width?

Because the data are simulated, the true answer is known for every participant, so the estimates can be checked directly against it. To compare GRIN with maximum likelihood on your own or published data, use the Analyse page.

The full-scale check from the paper

We tested GRIN on 24,000 simulated datasets that it had not seen during training. Its estimates of the sensitivities (where each cloud sits) closely matched the true values, with a mean recovery correlation of 0.91. Its estimates of each cloud's tilt (the four within-stimulus correlations) matched less well, at 0.59.

The uncertainty ranges are reported separately for the two families, because they are calibrated differently. Ranges stated as 90% contained the true value 94.6% of the time for the sensitivities and 84.2% of the time for the correlations. The correlation figure leaves out the 32,000 correlations that are exactly zero by design, in the model classes where the features are independent. It is based on the other 64,000. A nominal 90% range on a within-stimulus correlation therefore behaves more like an 84% one. Pooling the two families averages errors that run in opposite directions and gives a figure near 90% that describes neither, so we do not quote one here.

These results come from the fixed research checkpoint reported in the paper, on test matrices from 5 to 500 trials per stimulus. The small live exercise below is for intuition and will vary from run to run. It uses the same network as Analyse.

2. Calibration: do the intervals hold?

An interval is only useful if its stated coverage is right. For each stated level of confidence, a 50% interval, an 80% interval, and so on, this plots how often the true value actually fell inside. A calibrated method sits on the dashed diagonal: its 90% intervals contain the truth about 90% of the time. Bowing above the line is under-confidence and below it is over-confidence.

How to read this

GRIN's estimates of the sensitivities (where each cloud sits) lie close to the diagonal. Its estimates of the correlations (each cloud's tilt) scatter more, because a weak tilt barely changes the table of answers. Maximum likelihood does no better on the correlations (average error 0.27 against GRIN's 0.26 across 594 test matrices), and The imprecision in identifying independence explains why. With fewer trials per stimulus the points spread out, because the estimates become less certain and say so. On the full test set in the paper, the sensitivity curve sits just above the diagonal (ranges a little too wide) and the correlation curve sits below it (ranges too narrow). These are the two errors the figures above report. The small live check here can come out differently, and it can make the correlation ranges look better calibrated than they were in the full test.
