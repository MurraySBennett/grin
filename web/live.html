Live task

Do the task yourself. Watch the fit update after every response.

An ordinary 2×2 identification task, run as an experiment rather than a widget: set it up here, and the task takes over the screen until you finish or stop. Nothing is simulated except the stimulus — the responses are yours, and the fit is the same network the R and Python packages ship.

Fitting a GRT model by numerical optimisation takes seconds, so it can only happen once a session is over. Here it takes a fraction of a millisecond, so the design can make a decision at every block break.

What happens

A welcome screen, then the stimuli and keys, then blocks of trials. Between blocks you see the fit so far — with every earlier estimate still on the plot, dimmed, so the convergence is visible rather than asserted. The task ends when the posterior reaches your precision target or the ceiling is hit, whichever comes first, and you can then take the data to the Analyse page.

Trials on which you miss the deadline are discarded, not scored as errors: a missed deadline is not a confusion, and counting it as one would bias every parameter. Esc stops the task at any point.

An identification task

You will see one shape at a time. It varies on two features at once, and each feature can be high or low — so there are four possible stimuli. Your job is to say which of the four you just saw.

Each one appears only briefly and then disappears, and you have a short window to answer. You will make mistakes; that is expected and it is what makes the data informative. Answer as accurately as you can, as quickly as you can.

Press space to see the stimuli

The four stimuli, and your keys

These are the four. The keys are arranged to match: the top row (E I) is the high level of the first feature, the bottom row (F J) the low level; the left pair is the low level of the second feature and the right pair the high level.

Rest your left hand on E/F and your right on I/J.
